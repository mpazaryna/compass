# First Engagement — The "Centering My Business" Curriculum

## Problem

Compass can serve a bearing and a connected client will run it. What has never
happened is the thing the instrument exists for: a real client, alone, on her own
device, working through real material over weeks and keeping what she produces.

Everything built so far was proven with the consultant in the room. The gates
authored so far read *"the owner confirms the synthesis"* and *"the owner accepts
the foundation statement."* Those hold when someone is watching. Unsupervised,
they are self-attested: a client and an agreeable model can satisfy them without
the client having done the thinking. The instrument has not yet been asked to
hold a line by itself.

There is also nothing to work *on*. A business owner returning across many
sessions needs territory worth returning to; the catalog currently holds one
bearing.

And nothing above a bearing exists. Real engagements are not one journey — they
are several that belong together, walked in an order, adding up to something a
bearing on its own does not deliver. Authored today, a set like that arrives as
loose entries in a flat list, with the relationship between them living only in
the consultant's head. The unit the client actually wants to pick has no name.

Finally, the deployment is open. The README calls it an early single-tenant dev
spike with no auth, which was acceptable while the only person connecting was the
person who built it. It stops being acceptable the moment work authored for a
named client is sitting behind that URL.

## Objective

Sheri connects Claude Desktop to Compass, authenticates as herself, and works
through **Centering My Business** — a named curriculum of five journey bearings
and a capstone, covering the core territory of running and growing her business —
on her own time, across many sessions, keeping every artifact in her own
workspace. She finishes at least one bearing end to end without the consultant
present, and the curriculum ends in a plan, a scoreboard and a 90-day checklist
that can be checked against reality later.

## Scope — the curriculum

**Centering My Business.** Five journey bearings, each grounded in the standard
business curriculum, each standing on its own, and together making one ordered
pass over her business:

| # | Bearing | Grounding | Question it makes her answer | Terminal artifact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Life cycle | Churchill & Lewis, *The Five Stages of Small-Business Growth* (HBR 1983) | Which stage is she in — and at the Success stage's fork, does she expand or hold it stable as a base for something else? | Her placement, the evidence for it, and the fork decided with reasons |
| 2 | Know your numbers | Financial statement analysis — the three statements read together | What do her profit, position, and cash actually say, and where do they disagree? | Her real figures, and what the three of them together tell her |
| 3 | Growth multipliers | Customer equity / CLV decomposition (Gupta & Lehmann) | Clients × average ticket × frequency — which lever is genuinely cheapest for her, and why would she do the work? | Her three numbers, current and target, the compounded result, and her reasons |
| 4 | The funnel | Goldratt, Theory of Constraints — the Five Focusing Steps | Where is the constraint, and has she got everything free from it before spending on it? | The named constraint, worked through exploit → subordinate → elevate |
| 5 | Premortem | Klein, *Performing a Project Premortem* (HBR 2007) | It is eighteen months on and this failed — what killed it, and which of those can she affect? | Ranked causes of failure, the actionable ones paired with a response |

**The order is argued, not assumed.** Placement first, because everything after
it is conditioned on which stage she is in. Her real figures second, because the
growth arithmetic is meaningless without them. The multipliers third, because
they name what moves. The funnel fourth, because it finds *which* of those
levers is actually the constraint and works it. The premortem last, because it
takes the output of the other four as the plan it attacks.

Individually, some of these are short. That is fine and is the point of the
curriculum unit: a bearing does not have to carry a whole engagement on its own
when five of them compose into one.

**On sourcing.** These are the questions a business program teaches an owner to
ask, and Sheri is an owner — nothing about them is proprietary or difficult. What
is scarce is being *asked* them, in order, by something that will not let her
skip. That is the delivery problem this instrument exists to solve, and it is why
these bearings are authored as question sets rather than lessons: the moment a
stage starts explaining a framework, it has become a document again.

What prompted the survey was a Robbins course Sheri paid for and is attending.
Fifty-eight photographed workbook pages were read and indexed page by page, and
that inventory mapped which territory was worth covering. The grounding above is
where each territory is actually drawn from; the workbook is not a source of
text, and the prompts, questions, sequencing, and gates here are ours. Two of
these five changed source as a result of the research — see `research.md`.

## Traceability — what this parallels, and where it deviates

Sheri owns the workbook and is sitting the seminar. She will recognise the
correspondence, so it is stated plainly rather than left implicit — and each
bearing's deviation is deliberate, both because the canonical source is stronger
and because the deviation is what makes this ours.

| Bearing | Workbook parallel | The deviation, and why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Life cycle | Life Cycle of an Organization examples (Kinko's, Apple, Nike) and *Where Are You Now?* — pp. 48–53 | Churchill & Lewis replaces the Adizes-style stage names. Their Success stage carries a real fork — expand, or hold it stable as a base for something else — so the bearing ends in a decision rather than a placement |
| 2. Know your numbers | *Keys to the Vault* / Lemonade Game and the Balance Sheet–Cash Flow relationship — pp. 85–90 | The Lemonade Game is a simulation with invented figures. This bearing uses her actual books, and the payoff is the contradiction between her three statements rather than a worked example |
| 3. Growth multipliers | Jay Abraham's *3 Ways to Grow a Business*, the exercises, *Compelling Reasons*, and the *10x10x10 Pledge* — pp. 62–69 | Grounded in customer-equity terms, and gated against real salon benchmarks. The workbook's uniform 10/10/10 is replaced by naming the cheapest lever for *her* — for a salon that is almost always frequency, via rebooking |
| 4. The funnel | *The Power of Optimization* five stages and the telemarketing case — pp. 95–100 | The workbook shows the funnel and a worked example. Goldratt supplies the missing procedure: identify → exploit → subordinate → elevate → repeat, with a gate that blocks spending before free capacity is taken |
| 5. Premortem | *The 8 Triggers of Crisis* — p. 101 | The largest deviation. The eight triggers are a checklist answerable in any order; Klein's premortem is an ordered procedure that produces ranked, actionable causes |
| Capstone | *7 Forces Implementation Plan* — pp. 117–120 | The workbook's grid (principles → action items → dollar value → deadline) is a sound shape and a format, not IP. Ours assembles what the five bearings actually produced, and every line carries a number and a date |

**Deliberately not covered.** The survey read the whole workbook; most of it is
out of scope for a single-location salon and stays out: strategic innovation
(pp. 57–61), the X-Factor material (pp. 71–73), the sales communication model
(pp. 75–79), limiting-belief turnaround (pp. 92–93), wealth and money-machine
material (pp. 102–103), and raving-fan culture (pp. 107–109). Several are strong
candidates for *later* bearings — retention and referral especially — but not
for this curriculum, which is about centering the business she has.

## What she walks away with

A curriculum that ends in five separate artifacts has not ended in anything. The
measured outcome of Centering My Business is three documents, assembled by a
sixth capstone bearing:

| Artifact | What it is | What makes it measured |
|---|---|---|
| **The Plan** | One page: where the business stands and the fork she chose, her baseline figures, the target, the constraint and the sequence of moves on it, and what would kill it | Every claim traces to a bearing artifact; nothing appears that she did not produce |
| **The Scoreboard** | The small set of numbers she will actually track — rebooking rate, client retention, average ticket, clients per month, and her margin | Each has a **baseline, a target, and a date**. This is the artifact that makes the engagement checkable ninety days later |
| **The First 90 Days** | An ordered checklist of moves against the constraint, drawn from the funnel bearing's exploit → subordinate → elevate sequence | Each line has a deadline, and the exploit items come before anything that costs money |

The capstone is a bearing, not a generation step. It does not write these for
her — it walks her through assembling them from what she already produced,
and its gate is arithmetic rather than agreement: every scoreboard row has a
baseline, a target and a date, and every checklist line has a deadline. A
capstone that hands over a finished document is the exact collapse this
instrument exists to refuse.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Sheri authenticates to the deployed instrument as herself; an
      unauthenticated request reaches no bearing content
- [ ] The five bearings above are authored, validated against schema v2, and
      served by the deployed Worker
- [ ] Sheri encounters them as **one named curriculum**, in an order, rather than
      as five unrelated entries in a flat list
- [ ] Every gate names something inspectable from the outside — a number, a
      named list, a sentence in her own words — rather than a declaration that
      she is done. Where an external standard exists, the gate is written
      against it
- [ ] The prompts are original authoring; no bearing reproduces the workbook's
      text
- [ ] Each bearing's README names the canonical work it is grounded in, and the
      workbook material it parallels, so the curriculum can be defended when a
      client asks where it came from
- [ ] Completing the curriculum leaves her holding **the Plan, the Scoreboard
      and the First 90 Days** — assembled from her own bearing artifacts, not
      generated for her
- [ ] The Scoreboard is measured: every row carries a baseline, a target and a
      date, so the engagement can be checked against reality ninety days later
- [ ] Sheri completes at least one bearing end to end in Claude Desktop, on her
      own time, with the consultant not in the session
- [ ] She returns in a later session, continues where she left off, and the
      earlier artifacts are still hers and still in her workspace
- [ ] Confirmed against the live deployed instrument, not only in tests

## Context

This is **M4 (SHE-8)**, the last milestone on the roadmap and the one every
remaining roadmap criterion runs through. It closes *"Sheri completes at least
one journey end to end and keeps the artifacts"* directly. It closes *"a client
connects to a deployed endpoint and gets only their own bearings"* by making
authentication part of the build rather than deferred work. And it exercises *"a
journey runs across more than one session"* under real conditions — not a test,
a person coming back next week.

It also introduces the curriculum: the first grouping above a bearing, and the
unit most future engagements will actually be sold and delivered as. The idea
backlog is a list of candidate bearings; what a client buys is more often a set
of them with a name and an order. Establishing that unit here, against a real
engagement, is cheaper than retrofitting it across a full catalog later.

Authentication is in scope because this engagement makes it unavoidable, not
because multi-tenancy is being solved here. One client authenticating is enough.
The envelope already carries an optional `client` field for scoping and nothing
consumes it yet; whether M4 begins to is a spec question, not a prerequisite.

**Sequence.** Sheri works this material after she has sat the course. Authoring
can begin before then, but anything written first is written blind to what she
actually takes away — so expect revision, and treat the first bearing as the one
that teaches the rest.

## Notes — open questions for the spec

1. **How a curriculum is expressed.** Three candidates, cheapest first:
   *(a)* lean on `unlocks`, which the schema already documents as able to gate
   across bearings — costs nothing, but the set gets no name and no sense of
   progress; *(b)* a `curriculum` tag on the envelope, which `list_bearings` can
   group by — cheap and reversible; *(c)* a third profile, `profile: curriculum`,
   whose steps are bearing references and whose gates are the artifacts those
   bearings produce — authored as a folder with a yaml like everything else, and
   served by the existing three tools. The spec picks one. Whichever it is, a
   curriculum should not require a code change to author.
2. **Authentication mechanism.** The outcome is stated above; the means is not.
   Claude Desktop custom connectors support OAuth, and there are lighter options
   — a bearer token, an unguessable path. The spec picks one and states what an
   unauthenticated caller sees. The failure message is an interface.
3. **What makes a gate checkable.** This is the load-bearing authoring decision
   and it deserves a stated rule in the spec, not a per-bearing judgement call.
   A gate that can be satisfied by agreement is not a gate when no one is
   watching. Two of the five now have external standards to be written against:
   the salon retention and rebooking benchmarks for bearing 3, and Goldratt's
   *exploit before elevate* for bearing 4 — a gate that will not let her spend
   on the constraint until she has proven she got everything free from it first.
   The spec generalises from those two into a rule covering all five.
4. **The multipliers/funnel overlap is resolved, but confirm it in the prompts.**
   The research settles the division of labour — bearing 3 produces the three
   numbers, bearing 4 identifies which of them is the constraint and works it
   through the Five Focusing Steps. Different question, same data. The spec
   should still confirm the drafted prompts do not ask her the same thing twice.
5. **Slugs and names are client-facing.** She reads them in `list_bearings` and
   picks from them — the curriculum's name most of all. They are product copy and
   the spec settles them.
6. **Standing bearings are out of scope — but the Scoreboard is their seed.**
   The schema's second profile models exactly the ongoing half of this
   relationship: targets with baselines, rhythms on a cadence, initiatives with
   milestones. That is the Scoreboard and the First 90 Days, one step further
   on. The right time to reach for it is once a journey has produced numbers
   worth standing over — which this curriculum now does by design. Not this work
   item, but the spec should avoid authoring the capstone in a way that makes
   the conversion harder later.
7. **Does the capstone need its own bearing?** It is scoped as one because that
   works under all three curriculum mechanisms in Note 1. If the spec picks the
   third — `profile: curriculum` — the assembly could instead be the
   curriculum's own closing stages, and the sixth bearing disappears. Decide
   Note 1 first.
8. **How much to author up front.** Authoring five bearings blind is a bet. An
   alternative is to author one — growth multipliers, the highest value for a
   salon and now the best supplied with external benchmarks — run it with her,
   and let it shape the remaining four. The curriculum can be named and shaped
   before its members are all written.
9. **Where each source is cited.** Bearing `source` points at this work item's
   spec, as Brand Builder's does — it is part of the envelope and is served to
   the client, so it carries no local paths. The canonical grounding is cited in
   each bearing's README, which never reaches a client and is where a curious
   client's question gets answered from. The territory survey stays outside this
   repository; the page images are not copied in and are not baked. The spec
   decides whether `index.md` is copied into this work item so the record
   survives the absolute path it currently depends on.
10. **Two citations are unverified.** Adizes' corporate life cycle and Porter's
   *What Is Strategy?* are named from memory in `research.md` and are not relied
   on for scope. Neither reaches client-facing copy without being checked.
