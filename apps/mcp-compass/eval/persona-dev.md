# Persona — Marco, agentic-software developer for nonprofits

A second fixture for the same bearings. Its job is to prove **domain
neutrality**: a bearing that only works for one of these two personas is not
neutral, it is just tuned. Run both against any general bearing.

Everything below is invented for testing.

## Who he is

- Independent developer, seven years. Builds agentic software — LLM tooling,
  internal automations — almost entirely for nonprofits.
- Works alone; brings in a designer and a second engineer per project.
- Four or five engagements a year, three to six months each.

## The work

- Takes a manual process an organisation has run on spreadsheets and email for
  a decade and turns it into something an agent can run.
- Grant reporting, volunteer intake, case-note summarisation. The unglamorous
  middle of an organisation.
- He does not build websites and does not do "AI strategy" decks.

## Who it's for

- Program directors and operations leads at nonprofits with 20–200 staff.
- Usually the person who has been personally doing the manual work for years
  and knows every exception in it.
- They arrive skeptical — burned by a vendor who promised a platform and left
  them with a login.

## What he thinks makes him different

- He will tell an organisation the automation isn't worth building. Twice last
  year he talked a client out of the project and charged for the assessment
  only.
- He hands over the system with the prompts and the runbook, in their repo,
  and will not build anything they cannot maintain after he leaves.
- He starts every engagement by doing the manual process himself for a week.

## Why he does it

- His mother ran a food bank on a spreadsheet she maintained by hand at night
  for eleven years. Nobody ever built her anything.
- He wants the work to hold up after he's gone — not a dependency, not a
  retainer.

## Numbers he knows off the top of his head

- Roughly six active clients, three of them returning.
- Project fees run $18k–$60k depending on scope.
- About half his work comes from a previous client referring him.
- Utilisation: he thinks around 60%, and he is not confident in that number.

## How he talks

- Precise about technical things, vague and slightly uncomfortable about
  positioning. Doesn't have a pitch.
- Reaches for concrete examples from projects rather than abstractions.
- Suspicious of marketing language; will say so if a question sounds like it
  wants a slogan.
- Has never written a brand statement and thinks the phrase sounds a bit silly.
